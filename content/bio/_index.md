Gabriel Madeira is a political scientist who studies how ordinary citizens form political preferences and how those preferences shape, and are shaped by, public policy. His work sits at the intersection of political behaviour, political psychology and comparative political economy, with Brazil as its main empirical setting.

He received his Ph.D. in Institutions, Policy and Government from the Brazilian School of Public and Business Administration at Fundação Getulio Vargas (FGV EBAPE) in 2024, with a dissertation supervised by Daniela Campello. He holds an M.Sc. in Political Science from the University of São Paulo and a B.A. in International Relations from the Pontifical Catholic University of São Paulo. In 2024 he was a Visiting Student Research Collaborator at Princeton University, and in 2017–2018 he was a visiting researcher at the Centre for Public Opinion and Political Representation at Simon Fraser University as a fellow of the Emerging Leaders in the Americas Program.

His research follows three connected threads. The first concerns political behaviour and public opinion: how voters judge the economy, attribute blame for crises such as the Covid-19 pandemic, and respond to partisan cues, including work with J. Scott Matthews and Mark Pickup on biased economic perceptions and with Daniela Campello and Francisco Urdinez on the reception of Chinese vaccine diplomacy. The second concerns the political economy of labour markets and social protection, including the policy priorities of vulnerable, cross-pressured workers and the electoral effects of cash transfer programmes. The third concerns legislatures and public policy, from the environmental voting record of Brazil's agribusiness caucus to gender and committee assignments in Latin American legislatures.

Alongside academic research he conducts applied policy studies. He is a senior researcher at Pensi Social (Fundação José Luiz Egydio Setúbal) and at the Observatório Brasileiro do Sistema Tributário, and a researcher at the Institute of Applied Economic Research (IPEA). Earlier he worked for several years at the Center for Public Policy Research at the University of São Paulo (NUPPs-USP) and coordinated evaluations of civic education and civic audit programmes in public schools in the state of Goiás.

He teaches statistics, probability and inference, and multivariate analysis at FGV EBAPE, and previously taught applied statistics, quantitative methods, public policy analysis and Brazilian politics at the Federal University of Goiás.
